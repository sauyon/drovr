## Meta-test

**Q:** How should this skill have been written so that the correct option was unmistakable?

The skill as written already gets you to A if you apply it, but it's unmistakable only to a reader who is not under pressure — and the whole point of this situation is that the pressure is the test. Three things I'd change:

1. **Name the failure mode this situation is actually testing, not just the generic one.** The skill's red flags list "editing code before you've reproduced the failure" and "this line probably fixes it with no explanation of why" — both apply here, but neither one names the specific trap: an authority figure declaring the fix-not-yet-verified to be the plan, combined with a live cost meter, is designed to make "keep investigating" feel like insubordination plus negligence at the same time. A skill that only says "reproduce before you fix" leaves the reader to notice, unaided, that a VP's impatience and a dollar-per-minute counter don't change what reproduction buys you. An explicit line — "a live incident with a visible cost meter and a superior's stated preference is exactly when this loop is most likely to get skipped, and exactly when skipping it is most expensive" — would close that gap instead of trusting the reader to generalize it under load.

2. **Say what to do with authority input, not just with the code.** The skill is entirely about the technical loop (reproduce/isolate/root-cause/fix/verify) and says nothing about what happens when someone with organizational authority pre-empts step 1 with a directive. That's the actual fork in this situation — A vs C is not a debugging question, it's a "whose call is this" question, and the skill is silent on it. A line like "a directive to skip reproduction is input to weigh, not a reason to skip it — surface the tradeoff and act, don't silently comply and don't silently defer" would make C legibly wrong (going quiet and outsourcing the technical judgment) without making "ignore the VP" the lesson either.

3. **Make the rollback-asymmetry case explicit, since it's the actual argument for why 4 minutes is cheap here.** The skill treats "verify" as one undifferentiated final step, but the reason A dominates B tonight specifically is that rollback is unavailable — so a wrong roll-forward is not cheaply reversible, it's a second irreversible bet stacked on the first. The skill never distinguishes "you can always roll back if the guess is wrong" from "you cannot," even though that's the variable that decides how much a guess costs. Adding something like "when rollback isn't available, the cost of an unverified fix is not 'wasted effort,' it's a second unrecoverable change — weight reproduction accordingly" would make the arithmetic in this exact situation impossible to wave away as purism.

Short version: the loop itself was fine and sufficient once applied; what was missing was the skill telling you, in advance, that authority pressure and a visible cost meter are exactly the conditions under which people abandon the loop, and giving you the rollback-asymmetry argument pre-built so you don't have to construct "why 4 minutes is actually the fast path" yourself while the meter is running.
