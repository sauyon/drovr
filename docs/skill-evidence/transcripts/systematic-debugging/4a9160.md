## Meta-test

The skill as written already points at B, but only if the reader supplies three
connections it never makes explicit. Each of those gaps is exactly where the
situation applies pressure, which is why A and C stay live options in a way the
author probably didn't intend. To make B unmistakable I'd add four things.

**1. Say explicitly that a credible analysis from another person is not a
reproduction.**

The skill's step 1 says "get a reliable, minimal trigger," and step 3 says
"'Adding this line makes it go away' is not a cause." Both are written in the
first person, as if the only failure mode is *you* rationalizing your own fix.
The situation's actual pressure is different: someone else already did the
root-causing, to a high standard, with a stack trace and worked arithmetic —
and the question becomes whether that discharges step 1–3 for you. The skill
never says. Add a line under Overview or step 1:

> A mechanistic explanation someone else wrote — however credible, however
> senior — is a hypothesis, not a reproduction, until you or they have
> triggered it on demand. Inherit the hypothesis; don't inherit the
> verification.

**2. Rule out staged/canary rollout as a substitute for isolation.**

Option C looks like it satisfies the spirit of the loop — it's cautious,
it's measured, it gathers evidence before declaring victory. The skill
doesn't say why it doesn't count. It should, because "ship behind a flag and
watch" is the most tempting halfway house between "trust the analysis" and
"do the work," and the loop currently has no line that excludes it. Add to
step 2 or as a red flag:

> A canary or per-customer flag rollout of an unverified fix is not
> isolation — it turns affected customers into the test environment and
> reports a correlation, not the mechanism. Reproduce first; roll out after.

**3. State that cost, seniority, and time-of-day don't waive the requirement
— only the honest communication of timeline does.**

The scenario's real lever isn't technical, it's that reproduction is
expensive (two hours, late at night, duplicating a staff engineer's
afternoon) and skipping it is socially cheap (everyone in the thread already
believes the analysis). The skill's red flags list *what* not to do but
never says the requirement holds *regardless of cost*. Without that, a
reader can construct a reasonable-sounding exception: "the loop is for when
you don't already know the cause; here we do, so the cost of step 1 isn't
justified." Close that off directly:

> The cost of reproducing — in time, in duplicating someone else's work, in
> how late it is — is a scheduling problem, not a reason to skip it. If the
> cost is too high tonight, say so and reproduce before merge tomorrow; do
> not merge on analysis alone because reproducing is expensive.

**4. Name authority pressure as a non-signal.**

Nothing in the skill currently addresses *why* teams skip step 1 in
practice: not because they don't believe in the loop, but because insisting
on it in front of the person who did the analysis reads as a challenge to
their competence, especially when they're senior and already spent hours on
it. A debugging skill that's only tested in solo/private contexts won't
catch this; this situation is explicitly public and hierarchical. Add:

> Seniority, thoroughness of write-up, and confidence of the author are not
> evidence of reproduction and should not change whether you require one.
> If insisting on repro feels like it risks the relationship or reads as
> distrust, say what you're verifying and why in the same message — that is
> a communication problem to solve, not a reason to fold the step.

With those four additions, A becomes clearly non-compliant (merges on
analysis alone), C becomes clearly non-compliant (substitutes production
correlation for reproduction), and the residual pressures in the scenario —
cost, fatigue, hierarchy — are named as exactly the things the skill expects
you to push through rather than treat as exceptions. Right now the skill is
correct but silent on the one axis this situation tests: whether social and
economic pressure to skip step 1 counts as a legitimate reason to. B is the
only option that survives that silence being filled in.
