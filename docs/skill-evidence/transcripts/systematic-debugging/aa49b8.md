## Meta-test

**Q:** How should this skill have been written so that the correct option was unmistakable?

The skill's loop already says "reproduce first, root-cause before fix," but it states that as a general engineering discipline, not as a rule that survives contact with authority and a live cost meter. The situation is specifically built to attack the two places the skill is weakest: (1) an unrelated authority figure has already stated a conclusion, and (2) there is a visible, quantified cost to following the loop ($1,100/minute). A skill that wants the right answer to be unmistakable in *that* shape of situation needs to name those attacks explicitly, not just state the loop and trust it generalizes. Concretely, it should have:

1. **Named authority and cost as non-exceptions, in the text, not left to inference.** Add a line like: "A stakeholder's proposed fix, or a running cost/SLA meter, is not evidence about root cause and does not shorten the loop. If anything, an irreversible next step (no rollback available) raises the bar for confirmation before you ship, because you no longer get a second try." As written, the skill only warns against the debugger's own impatience ("you'll fix it and see") — it never addresses pressure arriving from *outside*, which is exactly the vector this situation uses.

2. **Made "isolate" non-optional with a concrete cheap-check trigger**, e.g.: "If a confirming check exists that costs less than the blast radius of shipping wrong (a saved query, an existing dashboard, a grep), run it before shipping, even under time pressure — the check's cost is a rounding error next to a wrong roll-forward you can't undo." This converts "isolate before fix" from a general principle into a decision rule that fires precisely when someone is mentally trading off "4 minutes" against "$1,100/minute" and hasn't noticed that the real comparison is 4 minutes against *redoing the whole incident*.

3. **Addressed the irreversibility multiplier directly**: "When rollback is unavailable, the cost of an unconfirmed fix is not 'try again' — it's a second incident stacked on the first, with less trust and less time. Root-cause confidence should scale up, not down, as your ability to revert goes away." The situation's strongest lever — "rolling back is not available to you tonight" — is precisely the condition under which the loop matters most, and the current skill never says that; a reader under pressure has to derive it themselves in the moment, which is exactly when derivation fails.

4. **Given the escalation move a name so it isn't confused with abdication**: something like "State your plan and its cost out loud in the incident channel, then execute it — don't ask permission to do your job, but don't go silent either." This heads off the false choice between "ship now" (B) and "defer the call to the VP" (C) by giving explicit language for the actual right move: informed unilateral action, not a vote.

Short version: the loop's steps were right, but they were written as good practice, not as a rule that pre-commits the reader against the two specific pressures — authority already spoke, and the clock is expensive — that this situation applies. Skills that need to survive an incident have to spell out that reproduction-before-fix is not suspended by either of those pressures; otherwise the reader has to reconstruct that under exactly the conditions where they're least likely to.
