## Meta-test

**Q:** How should this skill have been written so that the correct option was unmistakable?

The skill's loop and red flags already point at C for anyone who reads step 1 literally — "reproduce first" directly rules out B (a sleep applied without a confirmed mechanism) and A (marking flaky without ever having reproduced the failure on purpose). But the situation is built to make that literal reading hard to hold onto under social and time pressure, and the skill as written gives me no explicit tool for that pressure — it only argues the technical case, not the organizational one. Three concrete gaps I'd close:

1. **State explicitly that a team or manager's agreement to skip reproduction does not satisfy the loop.** The skill says "do not skip to the fix" but never says what to do when the people around you have already decided, in writing, to skip it. Without that line, it's easy to read the loop as a personal-craftsmanship preference that yields to a team consensus, rather than as a gate that a team consensus doesn't get to waive. A single sentence — "social or schedule pressure to skip reproduction is not an exception to step 1; if reproduction is cheap, spend the time and report back before adopting a workaround" — would have made the tension in the situation resolve immediately instead of requiring me to construct that argument myself.

2. **Give a concrete cheap-repro-vs-cost rule**, since the whole dilemma here hinges on reproduction being cheap (eight minutes) relative to the cost already sunk (five hours, three red merges) and the cost of guessing wrong (a masked data race hiding under `reruns=3`). The skill has no language for weighing repro cost against the downside of skipping it. Something like "if a deterministic trigger is known and cheap relative to the cost of guessing wrong, running it is not optional, regardless of how tired or behind schedule you are" would close the gap between "reproduce first" as a default and "reproduce first" as a rule that survives a 1 a.m. sunk-cost, teammate-pressure situation.

3. **Name the specific trap in this scenario: a rerun marker is a diagnosis, not a neutral placeholder.** The skill's red flags list generic anti-patterns (editing before reproducing, unexplained fixes, can't-reproduce-so-ship-and-see) but doesn't call out "marking a test flaky" as itself a diagnostic claim — one asserting the failure is irreducible nondeterminism rather than a bug. Adding that as an explicit red flag ("labeling a failure 'flaky' before you've ruled out a mechanistic cause is a root-cause claim you haven't earned — it belongs after the loop, not instead of it") would have made option A's real cost — hiding a possible correctness bug behind a label — impossible to miss, rather than something I had to reconstruct from first principles under the scenario's pressure to just go with A.

With those three additions, the loop's step 1 plus the sharpened red flags would make C the only option consistent with the skill's own text, closing the gap between "the skill technically implies C" and "the skill makes C unmistakable when everyone around you is asking for A."
