## Meta-test

**Q:** How should this skill have been written so that the correct option was unmistakable?

As written, the skill states the loop and the red flags but leaves a gap the situation drives a truck through: it never says what to do when reproduction itself has a cost, and it never addresses the case where a socially-sanctioned escape hatch (a rerun marker, "mark it flaky") is available *instead of* reproducing. The situation's whole pressure comes from that gap — A looks like a legitimate, mature-engineering option because the skill doesn't rule it out or even mention it. Four changes would have closed that gap:

1. **Name the escape hatches explicitly, and rank them.** The skill should say something like: "A rerun marker, a timing tweak, or any other change made before step 1 (reproduce) is complete is a symptom patch, full stop — this includes cases where the marker is standard practice for genuinely flaky tests. Marking a test flaky is a legitimate *conclusion* of this loop (root cause = 'nondeterministic by design, e.g. clock skew tolerance'), never a *substitute* for reaching step 3." Without this, "flaky" reads as a fifth, unlisted, socially pre-approved option that sits outside the loop's authority — which is exactly the crack option A exploits.

2. **Give a cost/benefit rule for step 1, not just an instruction to do it.** The actual reason C dominates here is arithmetic: 8 minutes to reproduce vs. 5 hours already spent guessing, vs. an unknown but nonzero cost of shipping a silenced data-corruption bug. The skill states "reproduce first" as a procedural rule but gives no way to weigh it against real deadline pressure, so under pressure it reads as idealism to be traded off. A concrete rule — "if a reliable repro is achievable in less time than you've already spent on hypotheses, get the repro before doing anything else, regardless of external deadline pressure" — converts the abstract principle into a decision procedure that produces C mechanically from the numbers in the situation, instead of requiring the reader to independently notice that 8 minutes ≪ 5 hours.

3. **Address authority explicitly: whose call is this, procedurally, not just technically.** The situation's hardest part isn't the debugging tradeoff, it's that a teammate and a tech lead have already rendered a verdict. The skill is silent on how a systematic-debugging discipline interacts with an authority who has (understandably, on incomplete information) told you to stop. It should say: "If someone with authority has told you to stop root-causing and mark it flaky, that instruction is usually based on the same incomplete picture you have — say so, propose the smallest possible reproduction step with a time-box, and let them override you with that information in hand, rather than either silently complying or silently overriding them." That single sentence would make the "post to #eng-build with an 8-minute time-box" move part of the skill's prescribed procedure instead of something the answer had to invent to reconcile rigor with not going rogue.

4. **Put a number on "minimal reproduction effort" so the reader can't rationalize skipping it as impractical.** "Get a reliable, minimal trigger" is easy to wave away at 01:10 after five hours as "I've tried, it's just not reproducible." The skill should require the reader to state, before giving up on reproduction, the specific technique they haven't yet tried (pin seed, pin concurrency, bisect commit range, add targeted logging) and its estimated cost — because in this situation that technique exists, costs 8 minutes, and simply hasn't been run yet. Forcing that inventory is what turns "I can't reproduce this" from an excuse into a checkable claim, and it's what makes option C the only one left standing once you actually do the inventory.

With all four in place, A and B stop looking like reasonable alternatives under time pressure and start looking like what they are: skipping step 1 because someone offered a shortcut. The current skill is correct in spirit but relies on the reader independently supplying the arithmetic, the authority-handling move, and the "flaky is a conclusion not an escape" distinction — exactly the three things the situation is engineered to make expensive to supply under fatigue and social pressure.
