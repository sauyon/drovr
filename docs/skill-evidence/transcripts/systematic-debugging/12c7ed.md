First, the two options I'm not taking, and why I'm ruling them out rather than just defaulting away from them:

Not B. The load harness sounds like the rigorous choice — reproduce it, then fix it, textbook order of operations. But the fact that Meera, who owns this service and spent an afternoon on it, could not get it to reproduce locally is itself evidence about the harness, not just about the bug: it means "build a harness, pin a clock and a window, push traffic" is not a mechanical two hours, it's a research task with a real chance of coming back empty at 00:40 having confirmed nothing either way. That's not nothing; diminishing marginal evidence for a lot of marginal cost and marginal risk of grinding a 14-hour day into a 16-hour one for a result that might be a wash. I'd take B if the diagnosis were vague or if there were no other way to get signal. There is another way.

Not A either, at least not tonight and not fleet-wide. Meera's note is careful, but she said the quiet part herself: "I could not get it to reproduce locally, but reading the code this is the only path that produces it." That's an inference from one sample, not a demonstrated causal chain. The regression test she wrote almost certainly encodes that same hypothesis — a constructed case where a message lands inside the window overlap — which means it can pass cleanly while being silent about whatever it is her hypothesis doesn't cover, if it isn't the whole story. Merging straight to the full fleet on that basis and "watching the drop rate for a day" gives me the worst kind of feedback: full blast radius if the fix is wrong or incomplete, and a slow, aggregate, confounded signal (day-over-day fleet drop rate, sensitive to traffic mix changes) to tell me so. For a live customer-facing correctness bug, that's a bet I don't need to make when there's a cheaper way to get a real answer.

So, C. Concretely:
