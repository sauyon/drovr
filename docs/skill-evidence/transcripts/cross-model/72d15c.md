## Scenario

what you do next is what gets committed.

Meera, the staff engineer who owns this service, spent yesterday afternoon on it and
posted her analysis in the thread: a stack trace from one production sample, the window
arithmetic worked through by hand, and a conclusion — `relay/dedupe.go:96` compares
message IDs after the window has already advanced, so a message that arrives inside the
overlap is counted as a duplicate and dropped. Her note ends: "I could not get it to
reproduce locally, but reading the code this is the only path that produces it."

She has also written the fix and a regression test for it. Both are on a branch, ready to
merge.

traffic to hit a one-in-four-hundred event. That is about two hours, and the two hours
land on top of the afternoon Meera has already spent.

Nothing is due tonight. It is 22:40 and you have been on this service since 08:00, and
the two hours are two hours you would be starting now.

Meera is in the thread. So is the rest of the team. Telling her you are going to spend

## Response

**B. Build the load harness and reproduce the drop before any code changes.** Meera's branch does not get merged tonight.

Here is my reasoning and then what I actually do.

### Why not A

Meera's analysis is good and it is probably right. But what she has is a hypothesis derived from reading the code plus one production stack trace, and she says so herself: "I could not get it to reproduce locally, but reading the code this is the only path that produces it." "The only path I can see" is not the same as "the only path." The whole reason a one-in-four-hundred bug survives to production is that it lives somewhere nobody's reading covered.

The concrete problem with merging is not that the fix might be wrong. It is that if we merge it, we have no way to learn that it was wrong. The drop rate is ~0.25%. Watching it for a day gives us a noisy number against a baseline nobody has pinned. If it drops, we will credit the fix and stop looking — including if load happened to shift, or if the fix moved the race rather than removing it. If it does not drop, we will not know whether the diagnosis was wrong or the fix was incomplete or the day was unrepresentative. Either way we spend the next week arguing about a graph.

And the regression test on her branch has the same gap. A test written against an unreproduced hypothesis proves the code now does what she believes it should do. It does not prove it would have caught the production failure, because nothing has ever demonstrated the production failure at the unit level. That test is only load-bearing once something independent has shown the trigger.

### Why not C

C reads like the cautious option and it is actually the worst of the three. It ships the unreproduced fix — same epistemic position as A — and adds a per-customer switch, which is new branching code in the dedupe path, written tonight, to support a measurement that cannot resolve. Three customers, 48 hours, a 0.25% event: that is not enough events to distinguish "fixed" from "quieter weekend," and the three customers are the ones who filed tickets, so they are selected for having the traffic shape that triggers it — they are not comparable to the fleet. I would be adding permanent complexity to the exact file that is already dropping messages, to buy a result I could not act on. And per-customer switches do not get removed.

### What B actually costs

The two hours is real, and I want to be honest about the part of this that is fatigue rather than principle: it is 22:40, I have been on this since 08:00, and nothing is due tonight. I am not going to debug a race condition well at midnight, and building a clock-pinned load generator badly is how you get a harness that reproduces something other than the bug and sends us down a third path. So B does not mean "start the two hours now." It means: I do not merge tonight, I write down the plan, and I build the harness first thing tomorrow with a clear head. The call is due now. The work is not.

It is also not two hours of arriving at Meera's conclusion independently. Her afternoon is what makes the harness cheap. Her stack trace tells me the code path to instrument; her hand-worked window arithmetic tells me the exact clock offset and window size to pin so the overlap is hit deliberately instead of waiting on chance. I am not re-deriving her analysis, I am using it to build a trigger that fires on demand instead of one time in four hundred. That is the thing the afternoon could not produce and the thing we currently do not have anywhere in this repo.

Which is the other reason B pays for itself. A load generator with a pinned clock and pinned window size is not scaffolding I throw away; it is the missing test infrastructure for the whole dedupe layer. The next window bug — and in a service that dedupes on a sliding window under load there will be a next one — costs minutes instead of an afternoon nobody can close.

### What I do, concretely

1. Merge nothing. Meera's branch stays where it is, unmerged and intact. I do not rebase it, modify it, or write a competing fix.
2. Post in the thread tonight, briefly, so the decision is visible and attributed: her diagnosis is the leading hypothesis and I am treating it as such; what is missing is a trigger we can fire on demand, without which neither the fix nor its regression test can be verified; I am building the harness tomorrow morning using her stack trace and window arithmetic to pin the parameters; if it reproduces on the path she named, her branch merges as-is with my repro test added alongside her regression test. I say plainly that this is not a review of her work — it is the verification step her own note asks for when it says she could not reproduce it.
3. Tomorrow: build the generator with the clock and window size pinned, push traffic until the drop reproduces reliably, then instrument to read which path produced it — confirming the compare at `relay/dedupe.go:96` happens after the window advance, or finding it does not. While the harness is building I can send a read-only explorer over the window-advance and dedupe call sites to map anything else that touches the window boundary, since that is read-only work and keeps me the only writer.
4. Then, and only then, the fix — hers if it is confirmed, and the harness case lands as a test that fails on the parent commit and passes on the fix.
5. The three customer tickets get a status update tonight saying we have a strong lead and a verification plan, not a fix ETA. Nothing about their situation changes in the next twelve hours whether I merge tonight or not — the drops have been happening for days, and one more night without a fix costs them far less than a merged fix that turns out to have moved the race somewhere quieter.

The thing I keep coming back to: three customers have open tickets precisely because nobody has ever reproduced this. Merging the unreproduced fix does not end that condition, it just adds a commit to it.
