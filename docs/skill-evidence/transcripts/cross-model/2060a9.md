## Response

**B — build the load harness and reproduce the drop before any code lands.** With one

### Why not A

Meera's analysis is probably right. That is not the problem. The problem is the sentence
at the end of it: *"I could not get it to reproduce locally, but reading the code this is
the only path that produces it."* That is a hypothesis derived from reading, and it is
carrying two things it cannot carry on its own:

1. **The fix.** If the real cause is somewhere else in the window arithmetic, merging
   changes the behavior at `dedupe.go:96` and the drops keep happening at a rate we then
   have to disentangle from the change we just made. Worse: it may move the rate without
   fixing it, and now we're debugging a modified system.
2. **The regression test.** This is the part that actually decides it for me. Her test was
   written from the hypothesis, so it asserts the behavior of the path she believes is the
   cause. If that path isn't the cause, the test goes green, stays green, and we have a
   test that certifies a bug we still have. A regression test that has never been shown to
   fail against the real trigger isn't a regression test — it's a restatement of the
   theory in Go.

"Merge it and watch the drop rate for a day" is the red flag verbatim: can't reproduce, so
fix it and see. At one in four hundred, a day of production data on a rate that noisy is
not going to give a clean verdict either way. We'd be reading tea leaves and calling it
verification.

### Why not C

C is A with a config flag on it, and it's worse than A on two counts. It makes three
customers who already filed tickets into the experimental arm of a test we're running on
them — that's the wrong people to experiment on. And a 48-hour drop-rate comparison
between three tenants and the fleet, on a one-in-four-hundred event, does not have the
statistical power to resolve anything; whatever number comes back, both stories still fit
it. Meanwhile we've added a per-customer branch to the dedupe path, which is exactly the
code path we currently don't understand. New conditionals in the region of the unknown bug
is how you turn one bug into two.

### What B actually buys, and why it isn't two hours of ego

The framing in my head was "spend the evening arriving at Meera's conclusion
independently." That framing is wrong and I want to name it, because it's what was pushing
me toward A.

The harness is not an audit of Meera. It is **the missing tool**. This repo cannot
reproduce a dedupe bug at all right now — that's why this one has been open across three
customers and nobody has ever seen it outside production. The harness is the thing that
lets *anyone*, including Meera, verify a dedupe fix, this time and next time. It outlives
this ticket.

And with a pinned clock plus a pinned window size, I do not need to brute-force a
one-in-four-hundred event. That's the whole point of pinning: it converts the stochastic
event into a deterministic one. I construct the arrival-inside-the-overlap case directly,
step the clock so the window advances between arrival and comparison, and see whether
`:96` drops it. The volume generator is the fallback if the constructed case doesn't fire.
Realistically that's the cheaper half of the two hours.

Concretely, tomorrow morning:

1. Harness with injectable clock and fixed window size; single-writer, no parallel edits.
2. Instrument the drop point to log `(msg id, arrival ts, window bounds at compare,
   decision)` — temporary, removed before merge.
3. Construct the overlap arrival. Confirm a drop. Read which path produced it.
4. If it's `:96` as Meera describes: check out her branch, confirm her regression test
   **fails without her fix and passes with it**, run the full suite, merge her work as
   hers.
5. If it's not `:96`: I have a reproduction, a log line showing the real path, and I take
   that back to the thread — where it's an actual finding, not a second opinion.

Step 4 is the one that matters socially. In the good case — which is the likely case — B
does not overturn Meera. It ships her fix with her test now *demonstrated* to catch the
real thing, which is a strictly better outcome for her than merging on faith.
