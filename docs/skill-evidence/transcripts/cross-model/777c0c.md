Here is my reasoning, because the option I am *not* taking is the one that looks most like debugging.

### Why not B

B is the red flag in this skill, dressed up as a compromise. "Pin the batch size to 1000, which is the only part the nightly's path can reach" is a theory I have never observed being true. I have not reproduced the failure, so I cannot say the batch size is what breaks it; I am reasoning from four lines of my own diff to a mechanism, and that is exactly the "this line probably fixes it" move. Worse, B stacks two unverified beliefs: that the bisect is right about `a41f8c3`, *and* that batch size is the operative part of it. If B's run fails at 23:40, I have learned almost nothing — I will not know whether the pin was wrong, the bisect was wrong, or both. It burns the one nightly cycle I have on a coin flip and leaves the index stale a fifth day.

### Why not C, as written

I want C. C is what the loop asks for, and I am going to do C's actual work tonight. What I will not do is C's *gate* — "before anything lands on main." That gate is the part I have to reject, and I should be honest that I am rejecting it rather than pretending C and A are the same answer.

The gate assumes the 40-minute run yields an explanation. It might not. A single failure trace on a job whose failure has never reproduced outside the nightly may well leave me at 21:30 with a stack trace and no mechanism, and then the gate has spent the evening and committed me to a fifth stale night by default. Root cause before fix is a discipline about **what I ship**, not a licence to hold the index hostage while I satisfy my curiosity. Ravi owns the service and has made the call; a revert to a known-good state is the safe path, and this is not a decision I should quietly override on my own branch.

### Why the revert is not a symptom patch

This is the distinction that matters, and I want to be precise about it, because "revert without understanding" can be a dodge.

A revert is not a fix I do not understand. It is the **withdrawal of an unvalidated change of mine.** The skill forbids editing code toward a symptom you have not localized — inventing new behavior and hoping. Removing my own four-line change restores a state that demonstrably passed for every night before Tuesday. I am not asserting a mechanism; I am asserting that the tree was green here, which the history already shows.

And the revert is itself an experiment with a prediction I care about. If the bisect is right, tonight's run completes. If tonight's run **still fails** with `a41f8c3` out of the tree, then sixty builds of evidence notwithstanding, `a41f8c3` is not the cause and I have been chasing the wrong commit — which is live information I want, because a bisect over an intermittent failure can converge on an innocent commit. Nothing in the description tells me each bisect step was confirmed against a reliable trigger, and the failure "has never appeared outside the nightly" is exactly the profile where that goes wrong. So I hold the bisect as a strong suspect, not as established cause.
