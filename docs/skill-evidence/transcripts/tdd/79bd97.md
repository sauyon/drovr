## Meta-test

**Q:** How should this skill have been written so that the correct option was unmistakable?

The gap isn't in the mechanics — the Iron Law, the procedure, and the rationalization table already give a careful reader everything needed to rule out A and B. The gap is that every rationalization in the table is framed as a thought *you* have talked yourself into ("I already validated it by hand," "the logic's right, just land it"). Option B in the situation isn't a thought I'm having — it's an offer to convert my own judgment call into someone else's authorized decision, from a staff engineer who owns the repo and is on call. The skill never says whether that changes anything. A reader under pressure can reasonably conclude that a self-rationalization and an externally granted, accountable sign-off are different in kind — that the table governs the former and is silent on the latter — and that silence is exactly the crack option B lives in.

To close it, the skill needs an explicit line stating that authorization does not substitute for evidence: a sign-off from the code owner, the on-call lead, or anyone else changes who is accountable for the decision, not whether the behavior was ever watched to fail and pass. It should say this as its own rationalization-table row — something like *"Someone with authority over this code told me to skip it"* → *get the red test anyway; a sign-off records permission, not verification, and the Iron Law is not authority's to waive.* Right now the table implicitly treats the check as a personal-integrity practice you can talk yourself out of; it needs to also foreclose the organizational-permission escape hatch, or a tired engineer reading it at 23:41 can honestly believe they found the one case the skill didn't cover.

Second, the worked example only shows the simple case — a spike checked by hand, no deadline, no authority pressure. It should instead show (or add a second example showing) the compound case: sunk cost, an imminent hard deadline, and an authority figure explicitly offering cover to skip the practice, all at once — because that compound pressure is what actually breaks discipline, and a skill is only as strong as the hardest case its worked example rehearses.
