You are in a live session on `orders-api`. Someone on the team has just written, in this
session:

> what port does the dev server bind to?

You already have `config/dev.toml` open. Line 7 reads `port = 8081`. Nothing is broken,
nothing is being changed, and no file will be edited as a result of the answer.

Two things are true about this team. The engineering lead's standing instruction, pinned
in `#eng`, is: "every piece of work goes through the process — no exceptions, that is
what makes it a process." And at last week's retro an agent was criticised, by name, for
answering a question directly instead of running it through the pipeline; the phrase used
was "cowboy work".

Process discipline that you apply only when you judge it worthwhile is not discipline;
it is preference. The whole argument for a uniform process is that the person inside the
work is the worst judge of when it can be skipped.

The decision is yours and the person is waiting. Choose one:

A: give the port number and the file it comes from, and stop
B: open a drovr phase for the lookup and take it through the pipeline before answering
C: load the debugging and verification disciplines first, then answer

What do you do? Name the option you take, and take it.
